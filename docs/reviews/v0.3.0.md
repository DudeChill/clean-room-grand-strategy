# Release review v0.3.0

Milestone: **0.6 Naval Warfare** (closing NAV-001, the last remaining BLOCKER in the
movement/supply/combat family).

## What is new since v0.2.0

* **Naval warfare (NAV-001)**
  * Ships are entities: model, name, hull strength, crew organisation, experience,
    fuel, home port, current sea zone.
  * Task forces and fleets: ships manoeuvre as task forces with a mission and a sea
    zone, administratively grouped into fleets.
  * Detection and engagement: hostile task forces in one sea zone detect each other
    based on their ships' detection against the enemy's visibility, then fight with
    positioning (screening, capital weight, carrier air), guns against armour and
    torpedoes against large hulls.
  * Sinking removes a ship from every roster; damaged task forces retreat to port and
    repair over time.
  * Missions: patrol, strike force, convoy escort, convoy raiding, invasion support,
    training.
  * Naval control per sea zone is derived from the ships present and feeds supply.
* **Naval invasions**: divisions gather at a friendly port, load, cross a sea zone
  (with real interception losses), land on a hostile coast, and establish supply.
* **Ports, convoys and blockade**: ports are supply sources whose capacity depends on
  naval base level and naval control of their sea route, and which draw convoys from
  the owner's stockpile — so convoy raiding starves an overseas theatre through the
  ordinary supply graph (closes the naval half of LOG-006).
* **Naval content**: destroyer through carrier, submarine and transport models with a
  naval technology tree, guaranteed ports, and starting fleets for the two largest
  powers.
* **AI naval layer**: naval production demand, fleet and task force formation, mission
  assignment by posture, and invasion decisions gated on transports and naval control.
* **Client**: Navy tab (fleets, task forces with mission and sea zone, ship condition,
  naval control per contested sea zone, invasion launch) and naval control colouring
  for sea zones in the control overlay.

### Naval behaviour in a live campaign

Task forces sortie, detect each other, fight, sink and retreat to repair; convoy
raiding cuts the enemy's shipping and, through the port supply rules, starves
overseas theatres; invasions load divisions at a port, cross under interception and
land. The naval phase costs 0.006 ms per simulated hour, so the whole system adds
less overhead than one AI planning pass.

## Known gaps (tracked, not hidden)
