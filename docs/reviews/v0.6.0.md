# Release review v0.6.0

Milestone: **trade and convoys** (ECON-001, MAJOR) — resources now move between
countries as real flows that industry consumes.

## What is new since v0.5.0

* **Trade routes**: standing agreements that move a resource per day from an exporter
  to an importer over a land route (through controlled or allied territory) or a sea
  route (port to port across a sea zone).
* **Real flows with real costs**: imports land in the importer's resource pool and are
  consumed by its production lines in the ordinary industry step; the importer ties up
  civilian factories and sea routes consume convoys from the stockpile.
* **Blockade**: enemy naval control in a route's sea zone scales delivery down, and a
  cut route is reported rather than silently guaranteed.
* **Route feasibility**: war closes routes, an exporter's surplus running out scales a
  route down, and an unaffordable factory cost shrinks or closes it.
* **AI**: an economic layer that opens and closes routes to cover deficits, scored by
  deficit size, factory cost and war state, acting only through commands.
* **Client**: the Diplomacy panel shows the player's resource balances (produced,
  imported, net) and every trade route with its partner, amount, delivery, route type,
  convoy and factory cost, with controls to start and cancel routes.

## Known gaps (tracked, not hidden)

* **MAJOR**: equipment designers, intelligence, multiplayer transport.
* **ECON-003**: the trade mechanic works, but the generated map's resource yields are far
  larger than industry demand, so only steel ever trades. Data tuning, measured and
  tracked rather than papered over.
* Trade is bilateral and automatic in its AI layer; there is no trade-policy
  negotiation, no resource stockpiling market and no lend-lease (DIP-007).
* Everything else is recorded in `docs/DISCREPANCIES.md` with severity and status.
